- ✉️ **Gmail: your mailboxes, read by the agents.** Connect as many Gmail accounts as you like through your own Google Cloud OAuth client ("Desktop app" type: free, no verification, and no callback page to host, the browser comes straight back to HydraOps). Each account is assigned to the agents that may use it. `gmail_search` takes Gmail's own syntax (`from:ana is:unread newer_than:14d`) and `gmail_read` gives a message, a whole thread, the labels, or saves an attachment into the task. Reading asks for no approval and costs nothing, but an email is other people's writing: what the agents read arrives marked as outside content, and anything with effects they want to do afterwards waits for you. Tools → Gmail has the step by step.
- 📝 **Gmail: drafts without asking, sending with your approval.** `gmail_draft` leaves a new message, a reply in its thread or a forward in Gmail's Drafts, with your files attached; nothing leaves the account, so it asks for no approval, and "read Ana's mail and leave me the reply ready" works in one go. `gmail_send` always waits for you: the card and the Telegram notice show the account, the recipients, the subject and the text exactly as they will go out, and only that message is sent, once. You choose per account what it may be used for (Read, Drafts, Send) when you connect it.
- 🔒 **A web page open in your browser can no longer reach the API from the inside.** Requests from your own machine need no token, and a page could ride that rule by pointing a domain of its own at `127.0.0.1` (DNS rebinding). The API now answers only to the names it is really reached by: an IP address, `localhost` and the machine's own name work without any setting; any other name gets a `421`. And only HydraOps' own pages may read the API's answers. If you reach HydraOps through a DNS name (a reverse proxy's domain, Tailscale's MagicDNS name), list it in `HYDRA_PUBLIC_HOST` in the `.env`.
